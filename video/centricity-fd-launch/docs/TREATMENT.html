01

What's wrong with the first cut

Your four notes, plus two I'd add before anyone else does.

  • "Text bahot simple tha" Captions were set once and faded up. No weight morph, no mask reveal, no relationship between the words and what the screen was doing. Type has to perform, not label.
  • "Effects hi nahi the" Every beat used the same two moves — panel rises, caption fades. A film needs a vocabulary of eight or ten named moves, each used where it means something.
  • "Screen khaali feel ho rahi thi" A single phone floating on flat cream, dead space on all four sides. The brand's own launch banner is full — 3D props, halftone texture, warm gradient, big two-tone type. We were ignoring the brand's richest asset.
  • "Music chahiye, beats par motion" There was no audio at all, so nothing had rhythm. The track is now the structure, not a layer added at the end — scene lengths are whole bars.
  • Mine: it was a feature list Four features in a row with captions naming them. No tension, no turn, nothing at stake. Apple never opens on a feature.
  • Mine: no reason to be 45 seconds The length came from adding beats up, not from a shape. 60 seconds, seven movements, one of them a silence.
02

The idea

One thought the whole film hangs off.

An MFD's client asks one question more than any other: "FD ka rate kya chal raha hai?" For years that question was where the conversation ended. The partner had nothing to show, nothing to send, nothing to book — so the client went to their bank, and the relationship went with them.

This launch isn't about a comparison table. It's about a partner finally being able to answer the question in their own name. That's why the emotional centre of the film is not the rate screen — it's the moment the comparison card lands in the client's chat with "Sent by: Somesh Nayak" on it. Everything before that beat is setup; everything after is proof.

Structure, locked to the track

Venue is settled: a looping film on a GFF booth. That decided the track, and the track decided everything below.

alt-cinematic-online.mp3 — REJECTED for this room52% masked · 9.0s hole · 69% of it below 100 Hz

Read the two charts together. The rejected track is the better piece of music — that dramatic shape is exactly what you want in headphones. In a hall it is the problem: the tall bars are where the audience hears something and the short bars are where they hear the crowd. The locked track is deliberately flat, and flat is the correct answer for a room you cannot control.

The cost, stated plainly: at 4.2 dB dynamic range there is no arc to lean on. The picture now has to carry every bit of the dynamics — cut rate, brightness, scale, density. The music keeps time; it does not tell the story.

04

What a loop changes

A booth loop is not a film someone sits down to watch. Four constraints follow, and they are not negotiable.

1

It has to work muted. Most booth screens are effectively silent — the crowd wins. Every argument the film makes must survive with the sound off, which means the type carries it and the music only keeps time. This is the strongest reason the treatment has no voice-over.

2

No slow open. Nobody sees frame one. The question now runs 4.3 seconds instead of sixteen, and the product is on screen by frame 130. Any ten-second window a passer-by catches has to contain a whole idea.

3

No hard ending. A film that resolves into black reads as broken on a loop. The last bar resolves and immediately re-poses the question.

4

The loop is the idea. The film ends on the wordmark and cuts back to another client asking the same question — and this time there is an answer waiting. The seam stops being a technical problem and becomes the point.

05

Art direction

Redirected. The warm-prop treatment is out; this is the premium product-film class — Linear, Stripe, Raycast, Vercel.

Cinematic product motion2.5D depth compositing Camera-ledMacro micro-interaction Kinetic type in negative spaceRhythmic editing Elastic UI / eased cameraPhotographic finish
DROPPED

The 3D prop world. Vault, coins, shield, money-plant. It came from your own launch banner, which made it defensible — but a banner has one second to signal warmth, and a film has sixty. Blown up, the props become the subject and the product becomes scenery. And every Indian fintech launch already looks like that.

THE RULE

The product is the set. No props, no illustration, no metaphor. The UI is treated as a physical object and photographed — floated in space, lit, moved through, shot in macro. Nothing appears on screen that isn't the product.

THE ANGLE

Warm light in a dark room. Almost every film in this class is dark UI on dark ground, keyed cool — Linear violet, Vercel cyan, Stripe indigo. Our app is light. So the panels become the light source: white UI burning in near-black, keyed with the brand's own copper instead of the category's blue. That combination is uncommon, and we get it for free from a design system we already refuse to change.

#0A0A0Cvoid — the room #141417lifted dark #FFFFFFthe panel — a light source #B69377key / rim light · DS token #D9A87Ebloom highlight #12B76Arates — glows #ECE7E1type · never pure white

Light

  • Warm copper key from screen left, raking across panel edges
  • Every panel border catches a 1px rim — that edge is what sells it as an object
  • Soft bloom on bright UI and on the green rates
  • Heavy falloff: the room is never fully lit, and we never see its walls

Depth

Every shot is composited in three or more planes with real Z separation, so camera moves create true parallax. Shallow depth of field throughout — usually only one plane is sharp. Rack focus is a cut we don't have to make.

Finish

  • Film grain over the whole composite, ~4%
  • Slight chromatic aberration at frame edges only
  • Vignette, always
  • Clean renders read cheap; this has to look photographed

Type

Urbanist, tight tracking, large, in platinum — set in the void beside the product, never over it. One or two words at a time, mask-revealed. The screens keep Montserrat because that is what the product is.

This also answers "screen khaali feel ho rahi thi" properly. The first fix was to fill the frame with objects. The better fix is depth, light and grain: in a dark cinematic frame, empty space reads as atmosphere, not as absence. Nothing gets added — the room gets deeper.

06

Motion vocabulary

The one rule that separates this class from everything below it, first.

Two motion languages, never mixed. UI elements are elastic — spring, slight overshoot, 200–400ms, because that is how interfaces behave under a finger. The camera and the world are eased — long cubic-bezier moves, 2–4 seconds, no overshoot ever, because that is how a camera behaves on a rig. Springing the camera, or easing a button, is the single loudest tell of amateur work in this genre.

The track, and what the audit found

Three tracks written, all three measured before a single frame was animated to any of them.

TrackDyn. rangeMaskedLongest gapTo fullPresence 500Hz–4k
bed.mp3 — LOCKED4.2 dB3%1.8s0.2s40.6%
alt-hall-b.mp37.7 dB7%4.0s0.8s10.2%
alt-cinematic-online.mp336.2 dB52%9.0s16.0s8.3%
REJECTED

The cinematic track loses 52% of itself to the room. Worse than the dynamics: 69% of its energy sits below 100 Hz, and booth speakers reproduce nothing down there. Roughly 8% of it would actually reach a passer-by. Its nine-second quiet passage — the best thing about it in headphones — reads as broken audio over a crowd.

REJECTED

alt-hall-b is muddy. 88.7% of its energy piles into the low-mids and only 10.2% reaches the presence band. In a reverberant hall that turns to soup.

LOCKED

bed.mp3. 4.2 dB range, 3% masked, full strength in 0.2 seconds, and 40.6% of its energy in the band that cuts through a crowd. 111.1 BPM, beat 16.202 frames, first downbeat at frame 9.9.

What the hall does

  • 75–85 dB crowd noise floor
  • Booth speakers roll off below ~100 Hz
  • Long reverb smears fast transients
  • Most screens end up effectively muted

How it was measured

Spectral-flux onsets, autocorrelation tempo, pulse-train phase, RMS arc, and a hall model — dynamic range, masked time against a −12 dB threshold, longest inaudible gap, time-to-hook, and energy per band. Ships at tools/audit-music.py; any replacement is measured the same way in one command.

Before I build

Four decisions. Answer these and I'll take the whole thing to a rendered cut.

  1. 1

    The idea. Does "the one question you couldn't answer" hold as the spine? If it doesn't land with you, the other two territories are on the table and I'll re-treat rather than patch.

  2. 2

    The redirect. Props out, product-as-object in — dark room, warm key, macro, camera-led. Confirm and I build to it.

  3. 3

    The track. Locked to public/audio/bed.mp3 on the audit above. Listen once — everything is pinned to it.

  4. 4

    Formats. 16:9 booth loop is the master. Confirm whether you also need a 9:16 vertical for social — the Share beat has to be re-staged for it, which is real work, not a rescale.

Once these are settled the order is: music first, then props, then the build — because everything downstream is pinned to the track. Nothing gets animated until the bar grid is real.
